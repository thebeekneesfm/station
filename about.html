About TheBeesKnees.fm

Old radio shows and 78 records, playing all the time on seventeen stations, with cartoons on the TV and ads from old magazines. Every station was on before you got here.

TheBeesKnees.fm was inspired by Spelunkr, a free TV network of forgotten Internet Archive video, made by one person. Go watch it.

How it works

You don't pick what plays. You tune in, and whatever is on is on, for you and for everyone else on that station at the same second. That's how radio worked.

It costs nothing. There's no account and nothing to buy. The only ads are the vintage ones.

If you'd like to help keep TheBeesKnees.fm on the air, you can tip the station on Ko-fi. It helps with the web address and upkeep. Every show stays free either way.

Whose recordings these are

Not mine. Every show and record plays straight from the Internet Archive, a non-profit library. This site hosts no audio of its own. Your browser plays the Archive's file, and "via archive.org" under each title opens that recording's page at the Archive, where you can see who uploaded it.

Listening here uses the Archive's bandwidth, the same as listening there. They run on donations, so if you enjoyed something, give them something.

This station isn't affiliated with or endorsed by the Internet Archive. Much of the radio collection was gathered and labeled by the Old Time Radio Researchers, and the 78s by the people who digitized them. The credit goes to them.

The cartoons on the little TV come from the Archive's classic cartoon collections. They play with the sound off, on the same clock as the stations.

The wallpaper is a real 1953 sample, scanned from that year's Sears wallpaper book (public domain, via the Internet Archive). The fireplace is footage of a real fire in Glen Lake, Michigan (public domain).

The sounds: rain on a window recorded by Laurent Guidali (CC BY-SA 4.0; the loop made from it is shared under the same license), a fireplace recorded by inchadney (CC0), and the radio-tuning sound by patolenin (via Pixabay).

The radio commercials between records come from the Archive's vintage radio commercial collections.

The vintage ads come from the Archive too: its Magazine Rack and its collections of old Australian and British print ads. See them all.

What's mine: the schedule, the drawings and the station breaks.

What's on

If something won't play

Recordings sometimes disappear from the Archive. When that happens the station plays static until the next show starts, so nobody falls out of step. I check for dead recordings and take them off the schedule.

If something shouldn't be here

If you uploaded a recording or hold the rights to one and want it off a station, get in touch with the station name and the title, and it will come off the schedule. Removing the file from the Archive itself is a separate request to them, and they explain how to make it.

Records released in 1925 or earlier are in the US public domain. Many of the records here are newer than that, from the late 1920s to the 1950s, and some may still be under copyright. The radio shows were broadcast between the 1930s and early 1960s and are widely treated as public domain, but not every part of every show is settled. If something here is yours, the request above is the fastest way to get it off.

What's kept

  • No account and no cookies.
  • Your browser remembers your last station and volume, on your own device.
  • The "listening" counts come from a random number your tab makes when you turn the radio on. It only says which station you're on, it goes away when you close the tab. Reactions you send carry only the station and which emoji, never who sent them, and no one, including me, can tell who it belongs to. The count runs on Supabase, which sees the connection while it's open. Nothing about you is saved there.
  • Your browser fetches audio from archive.org. The site itself is hosted on GitHub Pages, and like any host, GitHub sees your connection in its own server logs.

A note about the shows

These programs are played as they were made. Some are frightening, and some carry the attitudes of their time, including stereotypes that were wrong then too. Nothing is rated or filtered.
