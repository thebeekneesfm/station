The Bee's Knees

“Carefully contrived to relieve you from the four walls of today”

I made TheBeesKnees.fm because I wanted a place that would allow me to travel back in time. I've always loved the music, movies, and TV from the 1920s–1960s eras. I love spooky stories, noir, swing, crooners, sleepy waltzes. My favorite place to get lost has been in the Internet Archive (Archive.org), and I wanted a place to showcase all of my favorite parts of that world.

Given the internet is flooded with AI, it's feeling even more important to showcase true human creation. Thus, The Bee's Knees was born. No AI, and all of the website art is drawn by real artists.

TheBeesKnees.fm is free, there's no account, no ads (except the fun retro bumpers because they are just too fun) and it'll stay that way. Everything you hear comes from Archive.org, so if you enjoy the shows, a donation to them means the world as well! If you want to support keeping the station on the air, consider signing up as a member or throwing a tip!

How it works

Old radio shows and 78 records, playing all the time on fourteen stations, plus a Christmas station from mid-November to early January. You don't pick what plays: you tune in, and whatever is on is on, for everyone on that station at the same second. That's how radio worked.

  • Tune in by clicking a station card or any station in the listings. The listings show what's on now and what's up next.
  • Tap the title of whatever's playing to open that recording at the Internet Archive, and Recently played shows what came before it.
  • Rain, fire and storm: slide any of them up to play under the radio, on any station. Mix all three if you like. Your mix is remembered for next time.
  • Sleep timer: tap the alarm clock to have the radio fade out and turn itself off after a while.
  • The little TV plays old cartoons with the sound off, on the same clock for everyone.
  • The Sunday Funnies: a new full-page Sunday comic every Sunday. Tap it to read it big, pinch to zoom, and flip back through past weeks.
  • The comic and magazine racks: Golden Age comic books and old magazines to read page by page, on shelves by kind. Open the comic rack or the magazine rack. Each one remembers your page.
  • A word from our sponsors: vintage print ads rotate in the corner. Tap one to see it up close, or browse them all in the vintage ads page.

Keep it on the air

The only costs of running this site are the yearly domain, my time, and paying for original artwork, like the hand-drawn title cards. There's no advertising money and no investors, so the station relies on donations to stay alive. If you'd like to help, you can become a member or leave a tip on Ko-fi. Members get their name in the ticker, and Sponsors can put their name on a station for the month.

Whose recordings these are

Every show and record plays straight from the Internet Archive, a non-profit library, and this site hosts no audio of its own. Tap the title of whatever's playing to open that recording's page at the Archive. Much of the radio collection was gathered by the Old Time Radio Researchers, and the 78s by the people who digitized them. The station isn't affiliated with the Archive, and they run on donations, so if you enjoy something, give them something.

Records released in 1925 or earlier are in the US public domain. Many records here are newer, and some may still be under copyright. The radio shows are widely treated as public domain, but not every part of every show is settled. The same goes for the comics and magazines. If a recording, strip or issue is yours and you want it off the site, email me and it will come down. Removing it from the Archive itself is a separate request to them.

What's kept

  • No accounts and no cookies. Your browser remembers your last station and volume, on your own device.
  • Listener counts are anonymous: no one, including me, can tell who is listening.
  • The weather next to the clock uses your approximate city, which Cloudflare works out from your internet connection. It's used to look up the weather and isn't stored.
  • If you tip on Ko-fi and make it public, your Ko-fi name and message can show in the ticker for two weeks. Amounts and email addresses never do.

A note about the shows

I'm doing my best to remove overly problematic content, but please remember this is a time capsule. Some of these programs are frightening, and some carry the attitudes of their time, including stereotypes that were wrong then too. I'm here for the vintage vibes, not the values.

In this station we believe Black lives matter, no human is illegal, love is love, women's rights are human rights, science is real, and kindness is everything.

Station art

Credits
